## Fact sources
- Prelec, D. & Simester, D. (2001). Always leave home without it: A further investigation of the credit-card effect on willingness to pay. *Marketing Letters* 12(1): 5–12. Mean bids were $29 with cash and $61 with card for Celtics tickets. Card bidders paid by the next day, which rules out liquidity.
- Prelec, D. & Loewenstein, G. (1998). The red and the black: Mental accounting of savings and debt. *Marketing Science* 17(1): 4–28. This paper introduced the "pain of paying".
- Knutson, B., Rick, S., Wimmer, G. E., Prelec, D. & Loewenstein, G. (2007). Neural predictors of purchases. *Neuron* 53(1): 147–156. Insula activation to excessive prices predicted not buying.
- Thomas, M., Desai, K. K. & Seenivasan, S. (2011). How credit card payments increase unhealthy food purchases: Visceral regulation of vices. *Journal of Consumer Research* 38(1): 126–139. The study covered 1,000 households over 6 months.
- Soman, D. (2001). Effects of payment mechanism on spending behavior: The role of rehearsal and immediacy of payments. *Journal of Consumer Research* 27(4): 460–474. Card payers recalled their spending less accurately.
- Schomburgk, L., Belli, A. & Hoffmann, A. (2024). Less cash, more splash? A meta-analysis on the cashless effect. *Journal of Retailing* 100(3): 382–403. It pooled 71 papers and found a small but significant effect.
