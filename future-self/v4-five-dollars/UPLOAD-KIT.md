## 3. Fact check (for pinned comments or replies)
- Envelope experiment (Dunn, Aknin & Norton 2008): N = 46, $5 or $20, spend by 5:00 p.m. on yourself (a bill, an expense or a gift for yourself) or on others (a gift or a donation). The prosocial group reported more happiness that evening; the amount made no difference.
- Prediction survey: 109 students; 69 vs 40 predicted personal spending would feel better, and 94 vs 15 predicted $20 would beat $5.
- National survey: 632 Americans; personal spending was unrelated to happiness, prosocial spending was linked to more; people spent over 10x more on themselves.
- Bonus study: 16 employees, average bonus about $4,900; the share spent on others, not the bonus size, predicted happiness 6-8 weeks later.
- Toddlers (Aknin, Hamlin & Dunn 2012): 20 children around 22-23 months; happier giving treats to a puppet than receiving, and happiest giving their own.
- Replications: a 2020 registered replication found the effect when students actually bought for someone in need, but not when people recalled past spending. A 2022 close replication (N = 133) found no significant difference with the original analysis. The video says the effect is real but smaller than the headlines.
