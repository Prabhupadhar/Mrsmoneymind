# The Stranger You're Robbing — full script (v1)

About 5 minutes. About 760 spoken words at Kokoro's pace (about 150 words a minute).
Voice talks to the viewer like a vlogger. The screen never reads the sentence back:
it shows the moment, one pop word, or a reaction. Pop words are in **bold**.

Style: Psych2Go-inspired look, our own character (hair curl, mustard hoodie),
navy and orange scenes, handwritten pop words, gradient chapter cards.

## 1. The experiment (0:25–1:20)

| Voice | Screen |
|---|---|
| So, back in 2009, some researchers at Stanford did something kind of brilliant. | Chapter card: **part one — the scan** |
| They put people inside a brain scanner and asked them to think about three people. | Character slides into a cartoon scanner; screen shows three empty slots. |
| One: themselves, right now. | Slot 1 fills: young you. Brain icon glows bright. |
| Two: a stranger. Someone they'd never met. | Slot 2: "?" stranger. Brain icon dim. |
| And three: themselves… ten years from now. | Slot 3: older you. |
| Now you'd expect "future me" to look a lot like "me", right? It's literally the same person. | Character nods confidently. |
| Except… for a lot of people, it didn't. | Slot 3 brain stays dim; character's smile drops. |
| Thinking about their future self looked more like thinking about someone else. | Slots 2 and 3 slide together. **≈** |
| Your brain kind of… files future you under "other people". | Filing cabinet drawer labelled **OTHER PEOPLE** slams shut on old-you's photo. |

## 2. Why that wrecks your savings (1:20–2:15)

| Voice | Screen |
|---|---|
| And here's why that matters. | Chapter card: **part two — the charity problem** |
| Think about what saving actually is. | Wallet in hand. |
| You take money away from you, today, the person you can actually feel… | Young you hugging a dollar bill. |
| …and you hand it to a guy your brain treats like a stranger. | Arm stretches across a gap toward the faded old self. **a stranger?** |
| That's not saving. To your brain, that's charity. | Pop word **charity**, donation jar appears. |
| And nobody gives their whole paycheck to a stranger. | Character clutches paycheck, shakes head. |
| The researchers noticed something else, too. | Back to the three slots. |
| The people whose brains showed the biggest gap between "me now" and "me later"… | Gap between slot 1 and slot 3 widens. |
| …were the ones who'd rather take a smaller amount of money today than wait for more later. | Two buttons: **$ now** (small) vs **$$$ later** (big). Hand slams "now". |
| The more future you felt like a stranger, the less you looked after him. | Old self shivering in the cold, slightly faded. |

## 4. The twist (2:55–3:45)

| Voice | Screen |
|---|---|
| So, the researchers tried something a bit strange. | Chapter card: **part three — meet him** |
| They took photos of young people and digitally aged them. Grey hair, wrinkles, the whole thing. | Photo frame; a slider drags, the face ages. |
| Then they had people look their future self in the eye. | Young you and old you face to face; both blink. |
| And then they asked: say you suddenly got a thousand dollars. How much would you put away for retirement? | **$1,000** drops in; split bar appears. |
| In one experiment, people who'd just seen their older face set aside about twice as much. | Bar for "saw their face" grows to double. **2×** |
| Same people. Same money. | Two identical characters side by side. |
| The only thing that changed? For a moment, the stranger became a person. | Silhouette fills in with colour and a smile. |
| Quick honesty note: that was money on paper, not their real bank account. But the direction is the point. | Small "on paper" stamp in the corner. |

## 5. How to actually meet him (3:45–4:40)

| Voice | Screen |
|---|---|
| So how do you close the gap? Three things. | Chapter card: **part four — three moves** |
| One: see him. Use any face-aging filter and look at yourself at seventy. Don't laugh it off. Ten seconds. Just look. | Phone with aged selfie; 10-second timer ring. **see him** |
| Two: take the decision away from today-you. | Young you arm-wrestling himself, losing. |
| Because today-you will lose that fight every single time. | Arm slams down. |
| Set up an automatic transfer that leaves on payday, before you ever see the money. | Payday calendar; coin hops from paycheck into a piggy bank with a lock. **before you see it** |
| Three: promise him your future raises, not your current paycheck. | Raise arrow splits; part goes to old self. |
| There's a famous program called Save More Tomorrow that did exactly this. | **Save More Tomorrow** |
| People agreed, ahead of time, to save part of each future raise. In about three and a half years, their savings rate went from three and a half percent to over thirteen. | Bar chart grows: **3.5% → 13.6%**. |
| No extra willpower. Just a decision made before temptation showed up. | Character relaxes in a hammock. |

## Facts used and where they come from

- **Brain scan:** Ersner-Hershfield, Wimmer & Knutson (2009), *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience* 4(1): 85–92. Brain activity when thinking about the future self was more like activity for others; a bigger current-versus-future gap went with preferring smaller money sooner.
- **Aged faces:** Hershfield et al. (2011), *Journal of Marketing Research* 48: S23–S37. One online experiment: about twice as much put into long-term savings; working adults about 33% more. Hypothetical allocations, which the script says out loud.
- **Save More Tomorrow:** Thaler & Benartzi (2004), *Journal of Political Economy* 112: S164–S187. Average saving rate 3.5% to 13.6% over 40 months.
- **Dropped:** "write a letter to your future self". A 2020 pre-registered replication did not hold up, so it is not in the script.
