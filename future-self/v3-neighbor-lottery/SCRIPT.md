# The Neighbor Who Won (v1 script)

About 560 spoken words. Expect roughly 3:10 of voice, based on video 2's pace. Voice only (Kokoro), and each line has a **mood** (curious, shocked, firm, excited, sad, whisper, warm or neutral).
The narrator talks to the viewer. The screen never reads the line back. Pop words are in **bold**.
The look and characters are the same as videos 1 and 2: the kid in the mustard hoodie, navy and orange scenes, handwritten pop words. A new character: **the neighbor**, a friendly guy with a cap and a green jacket, who lives next door.

## Fact sources
- Agarwal, S., Mikhed, V. & Scholnick, B. (2020). Peers' income and financial distress: Evidence from lottery winners and neighboring bankruptcies. *Review of Financial Studies* 33(1): 433–472.
  - Data: one Canadian province, 2004–2014, prizes from C$1,000 to C$150,000. Postal codes have a median of 13 households.
  - Main finding (working paper WP 18-16): a win equal to median annual income (C$29,229) raised neighbors' bankruptcies by 6.59% over three years.
  - Bankrupt neighbors had more visible assets (house, car), but not more invisible ones (cash, pensions). Borrowing rose across the neighborhood.
- Kuhn, P., Kooreman, P., Soetevent, A. & Kapteyn, A. (2011). The effects of lottery prizes on winners and their neighbors: Evidence from the Dutch Postcode Lottery. *American Economic Review* 101(5): 2226–2247. Neighbors of winners spent more on cars.
- Luttmer, E. F. P. (2005). Neighbors as negatives: Relative earnings and well-being. *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 120(3): 963–1002. Higher neighbor earnings were linked to lower self-reported happiness, at the same own income.
