# Buy Time, Not Things (v1 script)

About 640 spoken words, 48 lines. Voice runs 3:28, the film 3:30 (Kokoro, `af_heart` mood blends).
The narrator talks to the viewer; the screen never reads a line back. Pop words are in **bold**.
Same look and cast as videos 1 to 4: the kid in the mustard hoodie, the neighbor (red cap, green jacket),
navy scenes, gradient chapter cards, handwritten pop words. New: a cleaner with a mop (the kid rig in a teal
shirt), a millionaire (old rig with a top hat) and a working mom in Nairobi (the kid rig in a magenta top).

Story arc: cold open with the $40 question and the 2% twist, then why time matters (time famine), the big
survey (with the neighbor as the "lazy?" foil and the shield twist), the causal experiment, the experts who
miss it, the honest Kenya test that did not go as the researchers bet, the 2022 check, three moves, and a
callback to the cold open's $40.

## Shorts (native 1080x1920, cut from the film voice; `tools/prep-short.cjs v5a|v5b`)
The three classic Shorts of 1-3 Dec already use study 9 (2%), study 6 (millionaires) and study 8 ($40, two
weekends). These two use different angles: the multi-country survey with the time-stress buffer, and the
Kenya replication test.
- **Short 1 (Thu 10 Dec), `v5-short1-neighbor-cleaner`, 33 s.** Lines 11-14 and 17-19. Hook (frame 0, on screen
  and spoken first): "Neighbor pays a cleaner? Lazy? Data: happier." (8 words; type: contrarian, with the neighbor
  as the second character). Stakes: you judge the neighbor. Story: 6,271 people in four countries, one question.
  Twist: time stress dragged life satisfaction down far less for people who bought time ("like a shield").
  Loop line: "So... still think your neighbor's lazy?"
- **Short 2 (Fri 11 Dec), `v5-short2-laundry-or-cash`, 34 s.** Lines 30-32 and 34-36 (line 33 cut for length).
  Hook: "1,070 moms. Free laundry, or cash?" (6 words; type: dilemma, number in frame 0). Story: ~40 h paid plus
  ~40 h unpaid work a week; three weeks of free services vs the same value in cash vs nothing extra. Twist: all
  three groups felt better, about the same. Loop line: "So, free laundry, or cash? The honest answer: it
  didn't matter."

## Fact check (26 Nov 2026)

**How the papers were read.** Lok & Dunn (2022) and Whillans & West (2022) were read in full from their open-access
JATS full text (Europe PMC). For Whillans et al. (2017), PNAS and PMC blocked direct downloads (Cloudflare and a
reCAPTCHA page; PMC's XML says the publisher does not allow full-text XML), so the article was read through the PMC
article page section by section (abstract, significance, every study's methods and results, Tables 1-2, figure
captions, discussion and limitations) with quotes pulled for every number used. The **SI appendix could not be
retrieved**, so nothing in the video relies on it: every number below is in the main text or its tables.

**Evidence strength, said out loud in the video.** The 2017 multi-country link is correlational (the script says
"link" and "a survey can't prove cause"); the causal evidence is one small experiment (n = 60, within-person, a single
day's mood). The large 2022 US survey found the link again but "very small" (d = 0.10), and not significant inside any
single income bracket (it was not moderated by income overall). The pre-registered Kenya experiment (n = 1,070) did
not find that time-saving services beat cash or a control. The video therefore ends on "a small nudge, not magic".
The three moves are suggestions, not findings.

**Not used:** Lok & Dunn's tentative finding that the link was stronger for people who value money over time (cut for
length; the authors call it tentative). Whillans & West's claim that "only 15%" of a representative US sample bought
time, and the "2% of working Canadian parents ... $100" figure, both cite other papers and were not checked.
